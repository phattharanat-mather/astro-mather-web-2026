We've seen it repeatedly: a team builds an impressive AI demo in a few weeks, leadership greenlights a rollout, and then six months later the project is quietly shelved. The demo worked. The product didn't. Why?

## The Demo-to-Production Gap

A pilot is optimized to impress. It runs on clean, curated data. Edge cases are handled manually. The team is fully focused on it. Latency doesn't matter because nobody is waiting.

Production is the opposite. Real data is messy. Edge cases are constant. The team has moved on to other things. Users notice every 200ms of unnecessary latency, and they blame the tool — not the infrastructure — when it's slow.

## The Four Failure Modes

**1. No feedback loop.** The model performs well in testing and then drifts. Nobody notices until the outputs are visibly wrong because there's no monitoring in place to catch subtle degradation.

**2. Human-in-the-loop theater.** Stakeholders feel better about AI output if a human "reviews" it, but the review step has no real process — it becomes a rubber stamp that adds latency without adding reliability.

**3. Underestimating integration cost.** The ML part takes weeks. Integrating it with legacy systems, auth, audit logs, and existing workflows takes months. Teams that budget for the model but not the plumbing get stuck.

**4. Optimizing for the wrong metric.** A model with 95% accuracy sounds good until you realize the 5% failure rate hits the same user segment every time — the one that matters most.

## What Successful Rollouts Have in Common

The teams that ship AI into production reliably do a few things differently:

- They treat the model as a component, not a product — and invest equally in the surrounding system
- They define a production-ready checklist before the pilot starts: latency SLAs, fallback behavior, monitoring dashboards, retraining triggers
- They pick a narrow initial scope and expand it — rather than trying to replace a complex workflow all at once

## The Right Question to Ask

Before kicking off a pilot, ask: "What would it take to put this in production for 10,000 users?" If nobody can answer that question concretely, the pilot will produce a demo, not a product.
